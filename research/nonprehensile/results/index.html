Results

From the scene alone, the planner finds the indirect lift — the block held at every instant — in 199 of 200 held-out searches (99.5%) and in all 34 of the original test set, with a learned value V₁ and ranker π.

The task

The block starts at rest on the floor and must end fully on the platform, at rest. The disk alone cannot carry it. The indirect lift tips the block onto the step corner, lets the disk walk round to the block’s raised underside, lifts the block off the floor and rolls it onto the platform, the block held at every instant:

{g} → {mx, g} → {mx, g, s} ⇒ {mx, gv, sv} → {gv, sv} → {mz, gv, sv} → {mz, sv} → {mz, t} → (transport) goal.

2026-11-19 … 11-21: the certified chain, the staircase, scene-generic learning

Four lines of work. Every number below is from a committed record in RobotControlLab/certified-contact-graphs (private); the commit or branch is given with each. Anything not yet final is marked pending.

The certified chain (issue #81, back half)

The chain is one route of the lift built as certified funnels (Certified funnels): each edge certified for every state of its entry set and every disk friction in [0.3, 0.5], each junction a containment of certified sets. The junctions now need real margins, and chain_check enforces them by default (ccg a6161ffa5):

  • a link where the control switches holds by at least one measured one-step 95/95 slack: the realized state’s change over one step at the hand-off; for the rest links, the hold’s own one-step change (decision 42 (a));
  • a boundary between two certificates of one law, with no switch, holds by containment with a margin above the float32 forward-error bound of the compared quantity (decision 43 (a′)).

Every narrow link from the tip through the landing passes, CHAIN PASS (narrow) (ccg branch fix/i81-landing-cover 2b7a20565, ccg/scripts/edge_tube/results/i81/back/landing_cover_swap/cc_narrow.txt; the links up to the push are those of master eddbd2aa8):

Link Kind Verdict
tip departure → tip funnel same-law boundary pass
tip funnel → 23° switch → pivot switch: slack pass, ≥ 1 slack
pivot → stop at 59.00° switch: slack pass, ≥ 1 slack
stop at 59.00° → gentle break rest: slack; force band pass, ≥ 1 slack; force contained
gentle break + make(mz) one certificate (a node, no junction) —
make(mz) → tcbreak departure rest: slack pass, +1 slack
tcbreak departure → tcbreak funnel same-law boundary pass
tcbreak funnel → tcroll funnel (62°) switch: slack pass, ≥ 1 slack
tcroll funnel → push switch: slack pass, +1 slack (decision 54)
push → landing (impact window) same-law boundary pass, above the float32 bound (decisions 46, 63)

The wide chain, from the post-impact rest set Srest through the c3 departure, the wide funnel and the goal stop to the release, passes end to end under enforcement (CHAIN PASS (wide), ccg c875eb515, i81/back/goal_band_juno524821/cc_wide_band.txt). Srest’s independent holdout: 0 of 773 rollouts outside (Clopper–Pearson 0.39%; ccg c5354ee66).

Commanded forces over measured bands (decision 44 (2)). Where a hold’s commanded force is handed from one certificate to the next, both certificates are now certified over the command ± δ, δ the one-sided 95/95 bound on |realized − commanded| normal force at the hand-off, measured on 1,536 rollouts of the realizer (calibration half 768, held-out half 768):

Junction Command ± δ Held out within δ Record
stop at 59.00° → gentle break 0.0311568 ± 1.07 × 10⁻⁴ N 756 / 768 i81/slack/stop_force_break.json
goal stop → release 0.15 ± 0.00859 N 757 / 768 i81/slack/rest_force_stop_release.json

At the goal stop the realized force lies one-sidedly above the command (0.1574–0.1586 N at both ends of the friction band). Both certificates hold over their bands (ccg 4313f9491, c875eb515).

The push, re-certified from the roll’s grown exit (decision 54). The roll → push junction had compared the roll’s exit with an entry the push tubes never certified (Issues). The push was re-certified with the same law from the roll’s exit grown by its slack: 16 of 16 sub-boxes hold, utilization 0.603–0.631, binding on progress (Juno 525614, ccg 13512e720). Roll → push now passes at +1 slack.

The same verdicts in float32. Every re-certified piece was rerun in float32 (fe32-poly) and holds with the same binding conditions: the lift funnels, the roll junction and both departures (ccg 5e679b9a9), the stop (8f15b80d4), the goal stop and the release (f7338e7b4), and the re-seeded push (utilization 0.598–0.626; eddbd2aa8).

The landing, certified (decisions 46 and 63 (a); branch fix/i81-landing-cover 2b7a20565, ccg/scripts/landing_motion/results/zono_cover_i81/). After the roll over the step corner, the disk pushes the block so that it lands on the platform while still moving. The impact window is the handful of 10 ms steps in which it strikes the platform and its contacts change. The certificate covers every state the push can hand over: the push’s 16 certified exits, each grown by twice the float32 bound, are split into units, and each unit is cut into pieces until one contact class (which contacts stick, slide or separate at each step) is proved on the whole piece, for every state in it and every disk friction in [0.3, 0.5].

  • All certified. All 16,384 units and all 138,286 pieces are certified, none left over (growth/cover/report.json).
  • Real margins, also in float32. Every contact condition clears its worst-case float32 rounding bound: the forward-error bound of the roundings along the code that forms the margin, scaled by the size of the quantities it is formed from and by the measured error growth of the solver’s own elimination (at most 5.49). The smallest margin is 1.0026 times its bound, the median 9,903 times. A float32 computation gives the same verdict on every piece.
  • The junction. The push into the landing passes the chain check (PASS push -> landing (impact window), then CHAIN PASS (narrow)): all 16 push exits lie inside the cover’s roots by more than the float32 bound.
  • Cost. Three passes on Ganymede2, 1,018 core-hours in all: the cover (607), the float32 check (325) and the elimination-growth check (86).
  • Running: the rest after the landing. The check that the block comes to rest on the platform from every state this cover hands over (the energy certificate, unit by unit) is running on Ganymede2; its result will be added here.

Scene-generic V₁ and π (issue #86)

The goal: a value and ranker that transfer to new scenes without retraining.

Step 1: S_step only (branch feat/scene-generic-v1, 76c41cffb). A transformer over the scene’s geometry as tokens (world frame, no positional encoding), trained on S_step’s 408 searches: 20 models (no augmentation, Augerino, Laplace, untrained; 5 seeds each). It ranks S_step’s held-out searches as well as the current model (π top-1 95.2%, 95.1%, 92.6% against 93.4%; V₁ Spearman 0.916, 0.920, 0.873 against 0.927). As predicted, no variant learns the step-to-step symmetry (invariant only if the P95 gap is at most the P95 seed spread; V in calls):

Variant P95 gap / seed spread, step-to-step map
none 7.45 / 3.52
Laplace 8.01 / 2.61
Augerino 6.19 / 2.34

Laplace’s marginal likelihood collapsed every candidate augmentation; only the far-field deletion (G6) collapsed as predicted, and the pre-registered predictions for the other five candidates (G1–G5) missed. Augerino’s continuous candidates collapsed and its discrete ones went to their maximum, G6 included (a miss). A hand-built symmetry also fails (4.94 / 2.14), so the bottleneck is the staircase’s unseen far field, not the frame. The misses are listed in the branch’s ccg/scripts/learn/scene_generic/README.md.

Step 2: a pilot family (branch feat/scene-generic-m2; in progress, cutoff Nov 28).

  • The first family, and the height limit it measured. The first pilot family (8 scenes by Latin hypercube, seed 7830, risers of different heights) ran its round 0: 450 searches with V₀ alone. Many stopped early, and the cause is physical: on a first riser of 0.41a or taller the disk cannot tilt the block up at μ = 0.3 (0 of 15), though it can at 0.5; risers up to 0.317a work at both. That family is kept only as the measurement behind this limit, not as training data.
  • The equal-riser family (decisions 57, 59b, 60). Both risers of every staircase are a/4 (31.25 mm), the height of S_step’s single step, so the scenes differ only in the width w of the first tread. Each riser is then the same local scene up to a translation, and that symmetry is exact.
    • Lower bound on the tread. The first tread must hold the block where it comes to rest after the landing: wmin = 1.266a (a + 33.2 mm), the 95/95 bound on the block’s first fully-on rest positions on S_step (305 rests; measured, not chosen).
    • The widths are the midpoints of 5 equal strata of [1.5125a, 2.5a]: 1.6112a, 1.8087a, 2.0062a (validation, F2m2), 2.2037a and 2.4012a. The stratum [1.266a, 1.5125a) holds the default staircase’s 1.5a and is excluded. The widths are deterministic, with no seed.
  • The direct step test (a diagnostic before the builds). Each step of the lift ran once from a settled staged start, on every scene at both μ (10 cases), with the default staircase’s V₀ as a stand-in:
    • the lower steps (the push to the riser, the pivot, the tilt-up and the regrip) succeed in 10 of 10;
    • the block lands on the first tread in 9 of 10;
    • it ends fully on within the budget in 4 of 10; the others run out of budget, or stop a few millimeters short where the stand-in V₀’s transport targets end.
  • The one non-landing is borderline (w = 2.2037a, μ = 0.3). After the regrip the block leans at 52.8° on the riser’s corner, its lower corner still on the floor and the disk at its lower back corner. The gate admits both ways on. Lifting the block’s corner off the floor fails in execution: the disk turns the block by only 0.1° and the corner stays down. Sliding the disk under the block’s bottom face turns it to 64.7°, but the disk ends 22 mm off its planned path and the judge rejects the call. Nothing else is on the route, and the search runs out after 8 calls. The same two moves succeed on the other scenes, from poses within 0.2–1.2° and under 1 mm of this one, and on this scene at μ = 0.5. At μ = 0.3 the lift-off near 53° is at the edge of what the disk can do; the physics allows the landing, and the failure is in the execution.
  • Stage A is running (decision 64): the validity tables and V₀ of the five scenes, on Ganymede2. Every build runs on one CPU model (AMD EPYC 9334), because V₀ builds on different CPU models differ (Issues).
  • Global placement, pre-registered. Round 1 runs each training scene at one random placement (seed 7915), so the learner can discover translation invariance from data; nothing imposes it.
  • The frame-local planner (decision 56 (a′)). A placed scene’s table and V₀ are computed in the scene’s own frame, so they are exactly translation invariant: F1g1p’s rebuilt table is identical to F1g1’s in every field, where the world-frame build had differed in 28 of about 1,124 single edges.
  • A learned-canonicalization arm, pre-registered: the same network on positions relative to a learned reference point (translation invariant by construction, labeled “architectural, not discovered”). It enters the online evaluation only if its 5-seed Laplace evidence separates from the world-frame model’s.
  • At the cutoff. If the pilot model solves the staircase’s top tread zero-shot, it is RSS’s lead learning result; otherwise RSS reports the top tread’s 0 / 30 as the motivating limitation.

Judge calibration for the RSS headline

The judge decides whether the block stayed held throughout a motion: at every step, each contact the plan keeps behaves as planned. A contact meant to stick must not creep, and a contact meant to slide must slide the planned way by the planned amount. The judge’s rule 3 measures each contact’s slip, per step and along the motion, and compares it with thresholds set per contact and quantity. The thresholds are measured, on calibration searches that run the headline’s own realizer (decisions 45, 49, 50 and 61):

  • The calibration searches. 180 fresh searches under the headline profile (principled-noinform), with the judge recording but not steering, from starts disjoint from the headline’s (seed 7920): batch 1 (Juno 524893; its 4 searches that crashed in the settle extension rerun on the fix, Juno 525149), batch 2 (Juno 525283) and batch 3 (Juno 525522). Every fourth start is held out.
  • A threshold needs at least 59 samples. A threshold is the largest value seen in the calibration half’s successful executions. The largest of n samples is a 95/95 upper bound only when n ≥ 59, since 0.95⁵⁹ < 0.05; a fresh motion exceeds it with probability about 1/(n + 1).
  • 34 of the 46 rows have a threshold: every contact of the block with the floor, the step and the platform, and the disk’s common contacts on the block. Held out, 10 of 16,836 per-call checks exceed their threshold.
  • 12 rows have fewer than 59 samples, and no threshold (decision 50 (a)): the disk on three of the block’s corners, and the disk sliding on the block’s front face, motions the calibration searches rarely make (n from 2 to 38). A move that needs one of these rows is refused, logged as “rule 3: uncalibrated (n < 59)”; there is no fallback constant. Over the calibration searches this refuses 359 of 17,291 recorded calls.
  • The unsettled first step is left out (decision 61 (a)). The searches started from geometric poses, the block exactly touching with zero velocity, so a motion from such a start began with one step of free fall. The record was recomputed without that step: no threshold changes, and the disk-sliding rows on the block’s back face lose 8 of their 566 samples. Searches now start from a state brought to rest in simulation.
  • The let-go window after a release: 84 samples (held out, 0 of 3,603 above).
  • The record: ccg/scripts/edge_tube/results/i81/slack_held_phases.json, md5 b7597fe6, branch fix/settle-start b28d6ae6d (in integ/principled), the decision-61 recomputation of fix/judge-recalibrate 5ebd7037a’s record.

The planner finds it from the scene alone

The search is given the scene and the goal, nothing else: every edit the gate admits except releases, transports toward the gate’s targets, and outcome children (a call’s child is the state it reached). Each edit that places the disk is offered at every placement along the face whose transition pose is a distinct node under the search’s own rule for identifying nodes. Every dynamic outcome is rejected, so every solve is held. The motions are the quasi-static realizer Λqs in MuJoCo, with its contact rules on (the local solver). Both test sets are held out (never used for training) and run at the band’s two ends μ = 0.3 and 0.5, each search up to 192 calls: 100 random lift starts (F1 seed 13), 200 searches; and the original test set, the canonical start (the disk 10 cm from the block) and 16 random lift starts (F1 seed 11), 34 searches. The ordering is V₁ (round 2) and the learned ranker π, trained on searches that offer every distinct placement, with V₀’s measured edge lengths.

Held-out set μ = 0.3 μ = 0.5 All (Wilson 95%) Calls to solve (mean / median)
100 random starts (F1 seed 13) 100 / 100 99 / 100 199 / 200 (99.5%; 97.2–99.9%) 82 / 83
Original: canonical + 16 (F1 seed 11) 17 / 17 17 / 17 34 / 34 (100%; 90–100%) 79 / 83

Every solve is held and is the indirect lift; 4 of the 199 hold the block by its lower corner instead of its underside. The one failure among the 200 runs out of actions to try. The canonical start takes 86 calls at both μ (11 on the path).

Every distinct placement against one placement per edit. With the edit offered only at the placement of largest margin and without the contact rules, the same search (round 1 or round 2 of V₁ and π) solves 193 of 200 (94 and 99) and 33 of 34. Paired on the same machines, every distinct placement with the contact rules solves 6 searches of the 200 that one placement does not and loses none (exact McNemar p = 0.031), and solves 1 more of the 34 (p = 1, run on another processor). Where both solve, it needs 81 calls against 88 on average. The two changes separate: without the contact rules, every distinct placement gains the same 6 and loses 2, which reach the budget (p = 0.29); the contact rules alone solve those 2 and lose none (p = 0.5), with the same calls (81.5 on average). The 7 searches distinct placements gain each pass through break sv at a further placement, a call that keeps the formation, tilts the block further and is kept as a progress child.

EULERDAMP off against on. All these searches run with MuJoCo’s EULERDAMP flag disabled, so MJX integrates the solved acceleration. With it on, MJX integrates a force sum that an unconverged contact solve can turn into a kick of a block at rest. On the same machines, the one-placement searches with EULERDAMP on solve 192 of 200 and 33 of 34 (193 and 33 with it off): 8 searches that fail with it on are solved with it off, and 7 solved with it on fail with it off (exact McNemar p = 1). Replayed call by call, three of the failures with EULERDAMP on come from such a kick (one of them the first push, which flips the block upside down), two of the solves with it on rely on the block’s creep at rest failing a call, and the others part at falls that end differently or at float-level differences that grow along the search.

Round 2 of V₁ and π, trained on 408 searches (seeds 20–29), ranks better offline (V₁’s Spearman correlation 0.87 → 0.93, π’s top-1 84% → 94%) but solves exactly the same searches as round 1: 193 of 200 and 33 of 34 on the same machines (exact McNemar p = 1), with 88 calls to solve against 89, never more, with one placement per edit. Its 7 failures among the 200 are exhausted candidate sets, not ordering failures. (With EULERDAMP on, the same held: 192 and 192 of 200.) With every distinct placement offered, round 2’s 280 searches were run again (273 solved) and π retrained on the same 408; V₁ is round 2’s.

The configuration (the defaults of master):

  • transition poses must be admissible: no disk target buried in the block;
  • transports aim inside their target sets by δ = 10 mm (the δ-erosion);
  • the block’s rotation range is ±180° (a ±90° joint stop could carry load);
  • each approach is checked by the gate’s hold test with the disk’s own force, and refused before the disk moves if it would tip the block (the same 33 are solved with the same calls without it);
  • unhinted search, dynamic outcomes rejected, 192 calls;
  • EULERDAMP disabled: MJX integrates the solved acceleration;
  • each disk-placed edit offered at every placement with a distinct transition pose;
  • V₁ of round 2 and π retrained with every distinct placement offered, V₀ with edge lengths measured from logged calls.

V₀ is the weighted graph distance to the goal plus a pose term (how it is built); its edge lengths come from the calls of round 0’s training searches (a transfer succeeds 46% of the time, a break 69%). V₁ and π are learned by fitted value iteration and the LTS loss from unhinted searches on other starts (F1 seeds 20–22, never the test set), in two rounds: 80 searches of 64 calls, then 48 of 192 calls run with round 0’s V₁ and π. V₁ enters only the search’s order; acceptance stays with V₀.

Clips

Each clip replays the search’s solution call by call on the processor that ran the search (AMD EPYC 9334), and every call reproduces the log bit for bit (43 of 43 calls). The clips come from the searches with every distinct placement offered, with V₁ and π, run without the contact rules. With them the canonical start at μ = 0.3 and start 1 take the same routes and calls; the canonical start at μ = 0.5 goes by the lower corner (86 calls). The typical and lower-corner starts are chosen by a script, not by hand: the most common route, then the search closest to its median calls on the path and in the search.

NoteHow to read a clip
  • View. From the side, with +x to the left, as in the paper. The green surface is the platform, the gray one the floor, the blue ball the disk. A red stripe marks the face the block rests on at the start.
  • Caption bar. The run; the current edit k/n with its formations (σ → σ′); the call’s outcome and the state it reached; the phase; the formation the recognizer reads right now; and whether the replay matched the log.
  • Speed. The badge at the top left states it. The path plays as one motion: in real time while the block or the disk moves, easing to 3× while both rest. A call’s outcome stays on screen while the next call plays.
  • Card. The last frame carries the search’s summary.

What this shows, and what it does not

  • The lift, found and held, at both ends of the friction band, from the scene and the goal alone, on starts the learned parts never saw.
  • One scene, one task. The step and the block; other scenes (the families) are not run.
  • Budget. 3 of 200 searches (and none of 34) fail within 192 calls: two reach the budget and one runs out of actions to try.
  • The realizer. Λqs plans held poses, not stick–slip plans (the local solver).
