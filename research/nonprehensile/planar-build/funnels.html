Certified funnels

Each edge of the contact-formation graph becomes a certified transition: every state of its entry set, for every disk friction in the band, reaches the next edge’s entry set. Chained, the edges certify a plan as executed, including motions that use momentum.

The idea

The hold gate and the certificate over a whole box of poses certify that the block can be held at every pose along a plan. A funnel certifies the motion itself:

  • Entry set. A box of states the edge may start from: pose, velocity, contact offsets, and μdisk ∈ [0.3, 0.5].
  • Law and tube. A feedback law and a tube around its nominal motion.
  • Certificate. For every state in the entry set and every friction in the band, the motion stays in the tube, the contact forces stay inside their cones and above a derived floor, the motor stays in its box, and the edge ends inside the next edge’s entry set.
  • Check. The certified edge is executed in MJX from measured states, and the measured coverage is reported at 95/95.

Edges whose sets compose this way form a chain that is certified end to end. The block no longer has to be held at every instant, so rolls, landings in motion and coasting become plannable edges.

Track C: a chain with a landing in motion

The chain tips the block, pivots it on the step corner, breaks and remakes contact, rolls it over the corner, pushes it so that it lands on the platform in motion, transports it into the lift goal, stops it there and lets go. Every segment below is certified for every state of its entry set and every disk friction in [0.3, 0.5]; the “utilization” is the certificate’s smallest remaining margin as a fraction of its capacity, so higher is safer.

In the chain’s own count all 14 of 14 segments are certified (the break and the make are two) and all 16 of 16 junctions between and inside them. One quantity is not in the rigid reduction and is measured in MJX: the disk’s clearance after it lifts off the block in the release.

How the edges compose. A junction composes when the certified exit of one edge lies inside the certified entry of the next; sets measured in simulation only validate coverage. An automatic check reads every certificate’s exported sets and tests each junction, and every one passes on certified sets:

  • the ten junctions from the block at rest through the impact window;
  • into the rest after the impact: the energy certificate’s units start from the impact window’s certified hand-off set;
  • out of that rest into the transport: the departure from the certified rest set, grown by the hold creep, is certified on 4 slices of the disk’s place k, and the wide transport funnel is re-verified from that departure set;
  • from the transport into the goal stop, which brakes at 0.15 N, and from the goal stop into the release;
  • from the release into the block at rest: the release’s passive braking ends in its own passive hold, the same pattern as the impact window ending in the post-impact rest.

Executed end to end. 768 rollouts at each end of the friction band. Each is one continuous MuJoCo/MJX simulation from the block at rest to the wide transport’s exit (its commands bitwise equal to the certified wide law), and a second MJX run carries every rollout on through the goal stop and the release; every law switches at its certified hand-off. The block reaches the lift goal, released and at rest, in 1536 of 1536 (Clopper–Pearson 95% [0.9976, 1]). Through the goal stop’s hold, 1528 of 1536 stay inside its rest set; the other 8 dip at most 1.1 µm below it at the first step, and all 1536 end inside it.

Why the chain ends with a release. A rigid model holds a pushed block perfectly still, but MJX’s contact is soft: under a held push its friction lets the block creep, 18–25 µm/s at 0.15 N, for as long as the push lasts. The certified way to end a plan is therefore to stop pushing. Once the block has stopped, the disk breaks its contact (break(mz)), and the block rests on the platform with no disk force at all. That rest is certified: the platform’s friction brakes any remaining slide at no less than 1.94 m/s², and every slip and tip is excluded. In MJX all 1536 rollouts settle inside the rigid rest set; through the disk’s retraction the block moves at most 24 µm and tilts at most 0.0055°.

The rest after the landing impact. The rest is certified by a dissipation (energy) inequality on all 16,384 post-impact units: friction at the platform and at the disk dissipates the kinetic energy faster than the hold force supplies it, which bounds how far the block slides and how far the disk slips on its face (Background: dissipation certificates). The certified rest set has xB ∈ [0.475, 3.666] mm and kmz ∈ [−9.608, +0.104] mm; per unit, the block slides at most 1.70 mm and k shifts at most 1.53 mm.

A barrier on the wide transport. A discrete-time barrier on the support load is verified over the wide transport through the support switch with a decay rate γ* = 0.0048 per step and no subdivision (Background: control barrier functions).

Record: a second variant of the chain uses a narrower transport (to 62.571 mm, utilization 0.208) and a goal stop at 0.094 N without a release; in it 11 of 12 segments are certified and the rest after the impact is counted as measured.

The roll, at 1°/s, is shown at 3× (the badge reads “playback 3.0×”), and so are the short stretches in which the block and the disk both rest, with brief pauses at some junctions; every other motion plays in real time. The badge at the top left always gives the current speed. The chain ends with the release: the disk lets go and the block stays at rest. The same rollout with the roll in real time.

The landing impact, certified with zonotopes

Every other piece above is certified by interval arithmetic (Background: interval arithmetic). Around the landing impact that fails: intervals store only axis-aligned boxes, every step shears and rotates the set, and re-wrapping it adds empty corners that compound (the wrapping effect). Splitting the boxes finely enough would have needed on the order of 236 pieces.

Carried as a zonotope, the set keeps the correlations a box throws away. Over the whole landing set the cover needs 310,372 pieces, all certified, none uncertified, computed in about seven hours on two clusters. Sampled trajectories (1.13 million step checks) and exact rational solves of the contact system (253,000) found no escape from the certified sets. The thinnest certified margin lies in a contact case that no sampled trajectory takes: an over-approximation, sound as it stands.

A set-based route: zonotopes

Background: zonotope propagation derives the operations below, with proofs, and maps each funnel check onto them.

The remedy is to certify a set shape that a step maps without re-wrapping. A zonotope is

c + G · [−1, 1]k,

a centre plus a linear image of a cube.

  • A linear step maps it exactly. It just multiplies G. A nonlinear step is linearized, its Jacobian multiplies G, and only the small remainder is boxed. In validated integration this is Lohner’s method, the first-order Taylor form.
  • Checks stay cheap. The minimum of a linear quantity a·x over the zonotope is a·c − Σ|a·G|. Force floors and friction cones cost about what they cost on boxes.
  • General polytopes don’t scale. Their face and vertex counts grow exponentially with the dimension, and a nonlinear map does not map a polytope to a polytope.
  • The hard part is the contact switch. At impact, part of the set is already in contact and part is not. The set has to be split along the contact guard and each part followed in its own mode. A hybrid zonotope, a union of constrained zonotopes indexed by binary factors, represents exactly such a mode-branched set (Bird, Pangborn, Jain and Koeln, Automatica 2023; for nonlinear steps, Siefert et al., ACC 2023).

The method in this chain. The funnel certifier runs the zonotope set type on every edge, the impact window included, and the edges certified both ways hold with zonotope margins equal to or above their interval margins. Interval arithmetic stays the rigorous subroutine for remainder bounds, implicit contact solves and rounding (Background: intervals and zonotopes together).

NoteWhere this leads

With the impact window certified, set-based certificates for contact-rich funnels deserve to be pursued well beyond this chain.

Justin Koeln and Justin Ruths at UT Dallas work on exactly these set representations: hybrid zonotopes for reachability of mixed logical dynamical systems, and the zonoLAB toolbox (arXiv 2310.15426). That line would be a natural collaboration.
