Search and ranking

Planned (step 4): a search over edits ordered by a learned ranker, measured in calls to the local solver.

NotePartly built

The plan was agreed on Nov 7, 2026. Since then the teacher search has run (Nov 8–9) and the CMGMP baseline has been evaluated; the ranker is not trained yet, and a redesign of the search, decided on Nov 9, is being implemented (below).

The search

A node is a simulated state, identified by its formation, the disk’s connected region and the box’s pose within a certified radius (Node identity). From a node, the candidates are the edits that pass the hold gate; each expansion calls the local solver Λ, which is the expensive part. The search is policy-guided (Levin tree search, or policy-guided heuristic search): a ranker gives a probability π to each candidate edit, nodes are expanded in order of depth divided by the probability of their path, Λ is charged when a node is expanded, and edits the gate rejects get π = 0. For this order, Orseau and Lelis bound the number of expansions by depth / probability of the solution. The theory note extends the bound to duplicate states (with the safe duplicate rule and the node key of Node identity) and to a Λ that fails at random (with restarts).

The ranker

  • Inputs: one token per contact template (type, gap, normal, contact-set length, whether it is on), one per body, and one for the goal; attention across tokens, with learned biases for pairs that are related by containment or share a body or an environment element.
  • Output: a score per candidate edit, normalized over the candidates into π.
  • Training: supervised, from searches that call Λ without guidance on many scene instances: the policy-guided-search loss on solution paths, plus a cross-entropy on whether each Λ call succeeded.
  • Baselines: search without guidance; the gate’s margin as the score; and a learned feasibility classifier with uniform-cost search on −Σ ln P (Hauser, Bretl and Latombe, ICRA 2005).

Scene families

Family Scene and goal
F1. Step, randomized step height, box size, friction, starts; goals on the platform; also lowering the box from the platform
F2. Staircase two or three steps of random rise and tread
F3. Wall and ledge topple the box against a wall, or lift it onto a ledge beside it

One disk for now. The families come after the redesign below works on the step, and a second disk after that (decided Nov 9). Validity is computed from the geometry for each family (Issues).

Teacher search: the first full run (Nov 8–9). The pipeline generates seeded step instances, runs the search with a teacher ranker, and logs every Λ call with the gate’s verdicts as training data for the ranker. Its first small check solved none; the transfers it failed were real realizer failures (makes that never reached the contact, disk transfers planned as a retreat, contacts placed at feature ends, hand-overs lingering in a tie), and the fixed realizer accepts 51 of the 76 it had failed. The full run (200 instances; the relaxed gate, the learned break skill, retries from other nodes of a key):

Task Solved Λ calls per solve
Disk contact on the box’s side (sub-goal) 40/40 5.1
Box flush on the riser (sub-goal) 40/40 17.5
The pivot formation (sub-goal) 39/40 18.8
Lift onto the platform 0/40 —
Lower to the floor 0/40 —

7,795 Λ calls (4,954 accepted) are recorded as training data (cmg/scripts/step4/teacher/results/t-full/merged/). Lifting has since been solved in 5 of 10 instances with a second learned skill (the local solver). Lowering is open: the only edit off the platform, onto the step corner, is a balance Λqs can’t hold (0/52, tried in the 11 instances whose box started 0.6–32.8 mm from the edge), and nothing brings a box farther away to the edge. Six of the 52 calls dropped the box into the lowering goal, in 3 of the 40 instances, and were rejected because they didn’t end in the requested formation; the redesign keeps such outcomes (below).

Baseline: the contact-mode-guided planner (CMGMP). Built from the authors’ code without root (in a container on Juno) and patched for the disk’s 1 N limit. Its plan for lowering, executed in our simulator and graded by the same strict test, succeeds: the box comes off the platform and rests flat on the floor.

Evaluated on 50 shared test starts (25 per task; 60 s per start on one CPU core, with restarts; settings chosen on 10 separate tuning starts; every plan executed in our MJX simulator and graded by the same strict test):

Task Plan returned Strict success 95% CI Median time to success
Lift 11/25 6/25 11–43% 45.2 s
Lower 25/25 19/25 57–89% 39.4 s

Lift plans are rare, and half of them fail in execution because the box doesn’t hold the goal; lower plans come easily, but 50 of 71 executions don’t hold the goal, and retries within the budget give 19/25.

Not yet a paired comparison. Our planner lifts 5/10 with the learned skills (7/10 with the proposed tie rule) and lowers none, but on different instances and with a budget counted in Λ calls. So far the baseline is ahead on lowering, ours on lifting. Our lift instances 0–9 happen to be CMGMP’s first 10 lift starts (ours 5/10, or 7/10 with the tie rule; CMGMP 2/10), but under different success criteria and budgets, so that is not the comparison either.

The paired comparison’s protocol (decided Nov 9; run after the redesign): the same 50 starts (25 lift, 25 lower); the same goal test and the same strict grading of every plan executed in MJX; a matched wall-clock budget per start on the same hardware class; no retries in our planner; Wilson intervals and McNemar’s test on the paired outcomes; both planners’ plan-found and executed-success rates reported.

Caveats: the baseline plans quasi-statically with a point finger, while execution is dynamic with soft contacts; moves between its planned contacts are tracked along straight lines without collision planning, so some execution failures may be the executor’s; its 1 N limit is applied per contact component (up to about 1.4 N along a world axis); and its contact-mode code was patched for the box wedged into the floor–riser corner.

Built (Nov 8): the validity tables for all three families. The builder now takes any polygonal environment and a rectangular box. It reproduces the step’s tables exactly, and built the two new families (default dimensions; random-instance ranges are documented, not yet sampled):

Step F2 staircase (2 steps) F3 wall and ledge

Nodes of W (recognizable, vertex–vertex only alone) 328 592 443
Excluded sets 560 pairs + 1 triple 1,624 pairs + 5 triples 840 pairs + 10 triples

New in the families: concave corners the box can wedge into (80 staircase formations, 42 wall-and-ledge), and pivot-like compound edges at every convex corner. Neither has collinear edges, so the “pit” case of the theory note doesn’t arise yet.

The test: a 2 × 2 table of transfer, holding out an environment family (F3) and holding out the number of disks (once a second disk is built), with cost measured in Λ calls. A targeted literature search found learned guidance for contact or mode search tested on held-out instances, objects, object counts or robot counts, but not on a held-out family of environments; the closest work counts solver calls with learned feasibility for pick-and-place (Ortiz-Haro et al., ICRA 2023).

The redesign (Decision 23, Nov 9)

Why. The search throws away motions that make progress but don’t end in the requested contact state. Asked to lift the box’s floor corner, Λqs instead tilts the box from about 10° to 67° while keeping all three contacts. The call fails, but the box is now where a short route lifts it without any learned skill: move the disk under the box, then push it over the step corner (the video).

What changes (implemented Nov 9 and now the defaults; evaluated on 40 lift and 40 lower instances, see the results):

  • Outcome children. A call yields at most one child, labelled by the state it actually reaches. The child is accepted if it is the requested state, or if it lowers a value estimate by at least δ = 10 mm.
  • Two new actions, each with its own gate: moving the box within its contact state, and releases, where the box is let go and comes to rest in the next state. Lowering becomes a push toward the platform’s edge, then a release to the floor. The final roll onto the platform becomes a release too, so the tie rule and the roll skill are shelved.
  • The order of the search is PHS* (Orseau and Lelis 2021), with its heuristic factor η from a value function, clipped to [1, ηmax] (ηmax = 10 to start; 4, 10 and 30 to be measured).
  • The value. To start, 𝒱₀: the distance to the goal in the edit graph plus the box’s distance to a fixed set of poses where the next edit passes the gate. Then one learned value V₁, the expected number of Λ calls to the goal, trained by temporal differences on every logged call, failures included (a call that succeeds with probability p costs about 1/p calls). The distance to the goal enters only as shaping and as an input.
  • Smaller decisions. The 125 mm gap screen (Near) is dropped: the gate and box transport decide, and distance only orders the candidates. The disk’s clearance check stays, as part of the gate. Duplicate pruning also compares calls per path probability. The approach lasts as long as its path needs. The settle clause is off for formation edits and required for releases and the final goal state. Retries (2 per node) are for the teacher only; the planner and every reported result use none. The search keeps popping batches, and a batched version of the call bound is being proved.

Since Nov 10 (decided with Aykut, one at a time):

  • The goal push aims at the goal window’s centre (default). Aiming at the nearest goal pose, the window’s edge, left boxes a few mm short and stuck: 15 against 13 of 16 end-to-end instances, paired, no losses.
  • The push gate (default): a box move is refused when no disk placement on the face can push the box along the planned path (a linear program at five poses). A paired check is running; shortening an unpushable push to its last pushable pose is built behind a flag.
  • Which ends count. An end with the disk outside its workspace (the joint ranges) is invalid; quasi-static runs reject ends reached through a dynamic event, and other runs tag and count them.
  • The value. V₁ and the ranker are retrained on the full lift goal (V₁’s error on lift-goal pairs 1.03 → 0.33 calls). V₁ enters only η, as 1 + k̂/g, behind a flag. Acceptance stays on 𝒱₀ with δ = 10 mm, which is derived from the merge radius, not chosen; V₁ takes over acceptance once it has a certified Lipschitz bound, and an exploration loop is to follow.

The release route, tested first (84 starts, the break states the teacher search reached; cmg/scripts/step4/release_test/, commit 12fc963):

From these: the approach now lasts only as long as its path needs (3c52753); the release realizer is the push with the “fully on” stop; the box counts as at rest within 1 mm and about 15 mrad (MJX shows a small periodic tilt cycle at rest, up to 12.5 mrad); simulator settings are being tested for the creep. One problem remains: the strict test rejects the step that brings the disk under the box (84/84), because the approach path brushes the box. The path is being fixed; how the recognizer reads such brushes is queued for a separate rethink.
