Decision 9: Xiao and Ji’s construction

Why the planar build now follows Xiao and Ji’s contact formations exactly, what we add to them, and what changed in the step chain.

Code. Branch phase1/planar of Symplectomorphism/c-gnn: commits 9261ece (code and tests) and af167f4 (compendium, Decision 9), Nov 7, 2026. The landing change below was accepted by Aykut the same day. Every number on this page comes from scripts/03_xiao_ji.py, run on the current code and on 84827c0, the last commit before the change.

In one paragraph

A contact state is a set of principal contacts (Xiao and Ji, IJRR 2001, Def. 1): contacts between surface elements that are not the boundary of other contacting elements. Our earlier construction followed that definition for the on-set but drifted from it elsewhere. Those drifts were conveniences rather than needs, and they are gone. We keep only three additions that their papers do not cover:

  • tolerances, to recognize contacts in a simulator;
  • several bodies, including a manipulator (the disk);
  • a no-contact state for each pair of bodies.

What changed

Before (84827c0) Now (Decision 9)
Environment Three convex bodies: floor, riser, platform. The step corner was owned by the riser, and the platform had an open end One object E, a polygon. Its features are its edges (E:L, E:S, E:P) and its convex vertices, found from the geometry: only the step corner vS = E:S/P. The concave floor–riser junction is not a feature
Contacts per pair At most one per body pair (convexity of each piece) At most one per convex element pair, and none alongside a contact it contains (their Def. 8). The box can touch floor and riser at once
Transfers Same or adjacent features on both sides: 92 pairs Xiao–Ji containment only: one feature stays, the other is an edge and one of its own vertices. 56 pairs
Compound edges “Two transfers in different body pairs” A set of single changes, all relaxing or all constraining (their Def. 9), kept only if every proper subset gives an invalid formation. On the step: the pivot and its reverse, 128 instances, as before
Hold gate The contacts that persist through the edit Statics of the edit’s less-constrained end: the same contacts, now with a name and a precedent
Valid markings 11,959 of 12,393 37,203 of 59,049 (several box–environment contacts at once)

Of the 92 old transfer pairs, only 48 were Xiao–Ji neighbors. The other 44 were not:

  • 20 went round a corner in one step. Examples: the disk moving from −xB to −zB, or the box rolling from one edge onto the next.
  • 24 changed both features at once, which their Def. 10 excludes.

Each of these skips a contact state that the motion really passes through. The disk now goes round a box corner in two transfers, through the disk-on-vertex contact. That closes the open question “one Λ call or two”.

Why Xiao and Ji, and not something else

A literature search (three searches; 13 papers read in full against our requirements) looked for a representation that is simpler and still meets our needs:

  • several bodies, including a nonconvex environment;
  • one local transition per edge;
  • recognizable from a pose with tolerances;
  • encodable for a learner;
  • extends to 3D;
  • supports a statics test on the persisting contacts.

None qualified. Everything simpler is coarser, and everything more principled is finer or impractical.

Coarser: they cannot see the pivot. A box tipping from its edge onto its corner keeps the same label in all of these:

  • stances of legged and climbing planners: a set of link–surface contacts with the location left continuous (Bretl 2006; Hauser, Bretl, Latombe, Harada and Wilcox 2008; Escande, Kheddar and Miossec 2013);
  • strata of active contact constraints, per body pair (Kyaw and Kelly, arXiv 2608.15541, 2026) or per designated point (Goodwine and Burdick 2002);
  • classes of free motion (Paul and Ikeuchi 1995: 18 states in the plane, with no feature or body identity).

Kyaw and Kelly assume each stratum has dimension n − |A|. That fails here: a box resting flat on its edge has a 1-dimensional set of poses, a box on its corner a 2-dimensional one, and both have the same single active body pair. With principal contacts as the label, the count comes out right.

Finer: one motion becomes several edges.

  • The cells of the configuration-space obstacle, labeled by their active point constraints, split a single slide along the riser top into two edges.
  • Kwak, Hasegawa and Chung (2011) triangulate every face, so their states depend on the triangulation.

The Leuven precedent. Meeussen et al. (T-RO 2007) use Xiao–Ji contact formations as the discrete layer, with the graph software from Xiao’s group. They then break each principal contact into point contacts (“we use the boundary points of the contacting area”) for the force model, as Lefebvre, Bruyninckx and De Schutter (TRA 2003) do. That two-layer structure is ours too: principal contacts for the graph, and the endpoints of each contact set for the hold LP. Their wrench models are frictionless; ours has friction cones and motor limits.

The hold gate’s rule has a name

Multi-contact planners test the smaller stance at a transition. In Hauser et al. (2008): at the transition the robot “can support itself only with forces applied at the contact in [σ]”, because “the applied force is zero at the instant of time when the contact is made or broken”. Hauser, Bretl and Latombe (2005) already drop the moving hold from their classifier’s input, “since the force exerted at this hold during transition must be zero”. Escande et al. (2013) require “the stability constraints of the smallest one” of the two stances.

Under Xiao–Ji’s neighbor relation every edge links a formation to one it contains, so “the less-constrained end” is always defined, and it equals our persisting contacts. Step 2’s choice 2 (use the old contact’s normal) now follows. In containment cases 2–3 the feature that defines the normal is the one that stays.

The landing changes

Before, ownership turned both sv and t on after the landing, so the landed box was {sv, t}. Under Xiao and Ji’s Def. 1, vS is a boundary element of the contacting platform edge, so sv is not a principal contact there. The chain is one edit shorter:

{g} → {mx, g} → {mx, g, s} → pivot → {mx, gv, sv} → {mx, sv} → transfer(sv → t) → {mx, t} → {t} → {mz, t} → transport.

Two changes from the old chain:

  • Landing is now a transfer. It goes from the corner to the platform edge that the corner bounds.
  • Sliding off the corner is now transport. It changes no contact.

Support is unchanged. t’s contact set ends at the corner, so the landed box is held exactly when its center of mass is over [0, x + a/2]. The oracle test still agrees with the LP. Cand offers every step of the new chain from a pose in its formation; the suite checks this.

A caveat to carry into the paper: genericity

Xiao and Ji’s theory assumes general position. They leave out degenerate contacts because “these PCs have zero probability of occurrence in practice”. They also enter “state splitting”, which needs two dimensions to be exactly equal, by hand as exceptions. With tolerance bands, such coincidences happen with real probability.

How we handle it:

  • Simultaneous changes. Our rule for compound edges (“every proper subset is invalid”) is the answer for simultaneous changes.
  • Ambiguous poses. A pose the tolerances leave ambiguous (a tie) is refused as a node and as a solver success. Xiao (1993) treats such contacts as “inherently ambiguous” for the same reason.

Ties rose under deep penetration. On 4,000 jittered demo poses there are now 475 ties (357 before):

  • 474 of the 475 have a contact deeper than εon;
  • in 136 of them sv loses to t, the contact that contains it (median penetration 3.5 mm), where ownership used to turn both on.

The demo itself still has 2 tied frames of 1,721. This is the known issue with εpen = 10 mm, fixed in step 3; the test’s budget is now 500.

Checks

  • The full suite, 68 tests, passes on Python 3.14, CPU (8:57) and GPU (13:46).
  • Gate equals persisting contacts. For make, break, both transfer directions and the pivot, the gate’s support points equal the persisting contacts.
  • Normals. For g → gv and s → sv, the contained contact’s normal equals the old contact’s normal at the flush pose.
  • Compound edges, brute force. A search over all sets of two or three single steps agrees with the implementation:
    • on every marking that has a compound edge;
    • on 1,500 random markings.
    Allowing up to four changes finds nothing new.
