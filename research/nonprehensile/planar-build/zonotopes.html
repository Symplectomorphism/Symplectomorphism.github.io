Background: zonotope propagation

Why boxes lose information when a set is pushed through a step map, what a zonotope is, which operations on it are sound and why, and how each check of a certified funnel becomes a check on a zonotope.

What this explains. Every certificate on these pages is a statement about a set of states: a box of poses on which contacts hold the box, a band of a certified funnel, an entry set, an exit set. Today the certifier stores all of them as boxes and pushes them through the dynamics by interval arithmetic. This page shows the two ways that loses information, introduces the zonotope, proves the handful of operations a certifier needs, and then maps each condition of our funnels onto those operations. You need a little linear algebra and the mean value theorem.

Where this is going. A zonotope set type is being built into the certifier’s core, and it is the planned replacement for the interval machinery. Boxes are a special case of zonotopes, so the replacement can happen one edge at a time. The landing’s impact window, where intervals fail, is its first client. The set representation for mode-branched sets is the hybrid zonotope of Koeln and colleagues, and that line is being pursued with them.

1. Intervals, and the two ways they lose

An interval \([x]=[\underline x,\overline x]\) is a closed bounded set of reals, and a box is a vector of intervals. Interval arithmetic replaces each operation by the set of its results, rounded outward: \[ [x]+[y]=[\underline x+\underline y,\ \overline x+\overline y],\qquad [x]-[y]=[\underline x-\overline y,\ \overline x-\underline y], \] and a product takes the least and largest of the four endpoint products.

Why it is sound. Evaluate an expression with every operation replaced by its interval version: the result contains the expression’s value at every point of the box. That is induction over the expression, since each interval operation contains every result of the operation on its arguments. This inclusion property is the whole basis of every interval certificate here, however coarse the result is.

Loss 1: dependency. Interval arithmetic treats each occurrence of a variable as a new, independent variable. For \([x]=[0,1]\), \[ [x]-[x]=[0-1,\ 1-0]=[-1,1], \] although \(x-x=0\) for every \(x\). Inside one formula the mean-value form (the value at the centre plus an interval gradient times the half-widths) removes most of this.

Loss 2: wrapping. A step of a dynamical system maps a box to a set that is usually not a box. The next step only accepts a box, so the image is replaced by its interval hull, the smallest box around it. The hull adds corners the true set does not have, and the next step maps those corners too. The loss compounds over the steps.

TipWorked example: a rotation by 45°

Let each step rotate the plane by \(45°\) and start from the square \([-1,1]^2\). The rotated square has its corners on the axes at distance \(\sqrt2\), so its interval hull is \([-\sqrt2,\sqrt2]^2\). Rotating a box of half-width \(h\) and boxing it again gives half-width \(\sqrt2\,h\), every step. After 8 steps, two full turns, the box is \([-16,16]^2\), while the true set is the original square again. The rotation is an isometry; the factor 16 in width (256 in area) comes from nothing but the choice to store boxes.

Splitting boxes (bisection) fights wrapping only by brute force. In the landing’s impact window it would need about 1010 pieces.

2. Zonotopes

A zonotope has a centre \(c\in\R^n\) and a generator matrix \(G=[g_1,\dots,g_m]\in\R^{n\times m}\): \[ \mathcal Z(c,G)=\{\,c+G\xi:\ \xi\in[-1,1]^m\,\}. \] It is a sum of \(m\) segments, so it is a centrally symmetric polytope. Three special cases:

  • A box is \(\mathcal Z(c,\operatorname{diag}(r))\) with half-widths \(r\). Every interval certificate is already a zonotope certificate with a diagonal \(G\).
  • A parallelepiped (\(m=n\), \(G\) invertible) is the set form of Lohner’s method for validated ODE solutions, which keeps a rotated coordinate frame from step to step.
  • A segment is a zonotope with one generator.

Affine maps and sums are exact

\[ A\,\mathcal Z(c,G)+b=\mathcal Z(Ac+b,\ AG),\qquad \mathcal Z(c_1,G_1)\oplus\mathcal Z(c_2,G_2)=\mathcal Z(c_1+c_2,\ [G_1,G_2]). \] Why: \(A(c+G\xi)+b=(Ac+b)+(AG)\xi\) for every \(\xi\), and stacking \((\xi_1,\xi_2)\) ranges over the bigger cube exactly when each part ranges over its own. Nothing is lost. A linear step costs one matrix product, and the rotation example is resolved: \(R^8\mathcal Z(0,I)=\mathcal Z(0,R^8)= \mathcal Z(0,I)\).

Affine arithmetic is zonotopes, one operation at a time

Affine arithmetic writes each quantity as \(\hat x=x_0+\sum_i x_i\epsilon_i\) with noise symbols \(\epsilon_i\in[-1,1]\) shared by all quantities. A vector of such forms is a zonotope: the \(x_0\)’s are the centre and the coefficient columns are the generators. Shared symbols cancel, so \(\hat x-\hat x=0\) exactly: the dependency loss is gone. A nonlinear operation becomes its best affine approximation plus one new noise symbol that bounds the error. A zonotope step does the same for a whole step map at once.

Nonlinear steps: linear part plus a remainder

Let \(f\) be the step map and \(X=c+[-r,r]\) the zonotope’s interval hull, with \(r=|G|\,\mathbf 1\) (row sums of \(|G|\)). Linearize at the centre and append the error as new generators.

Lagrange form. If \([H_j]\) bounds the Hessian of \(f_j\) on \(X\), then \[ f(\mathcal Z(c,G))\subseteq\mathcal Z\big(f(c),\ [\,J_f(c)\,G,\ \operatorname{diag}(e)\,]\big), \qquad e_j=\tfrac12\, r^\top\big|[H_j]\big|\,r . \] Why: for \(x=c+G\xi\), Taylor’s theorem gives \(f_j(x)=f_j(c)+\nabla f_j(c)\cdot G\xi+\tfrac12\,\delta^\top H_j(\zeta)\,\delta\) with \(\delta=G\xi\), \(|\delta|\le r\) and \(\zeta\) on the segment from \(c\) to \(x\), inside the convex set \(X\). The last term is at most \(e_j\).

Mean-value form. If \([J]\) bounds the Jacobian on \(X\), with midpoint \(M\) and radius \(\Delta\), then \(f(\mathcal Z)\subseteq\mathcal Z(f(c),\,[MG,\ \operatorname{diag}(\Delta r)])\).

The Lagrange remainder is quadratic in the set’s size \(r\), with the Hessian bound as its coefficient. The mean-value remainder is linear in \(r\), with the Jacobian enclosure’s radius \(\Delta\) as its coefficient, and it carries every overestimate of that enclosure; chained over several steps those overestimates multiply. In both forms the linear part \(JG\) keeps every correlation the set had, which is exactly what re-boxing throws away. Floating-point rounding is bounded and appended as one more diagonal block.

Order reduction

Every nonlinear step appends generators, so they have to be capped. Split \(G=[G_1,G_2]\) and replace the second part by its interval hull: \[ \mathcal Z(c,G)\subseteq\mathcal Z\big(c,\ [\,G_1,\ \operatorname{diag}(|G_2|\,\mathbf 1)\,]\big). \] Why: \(\mathcal Z(c,G)=\mathcal Z(c,G_1)\oplus\mathcal Z(0,G_2)\), and the second term lies in its interval hull. Girard’s rule boxes the generators that lose least when boxed, so the loss stays small and controlled.

The closed-form check

\[ \min_{x\in\mathcal Z(c,G)} a\cdot x \;=\; a\cdot c-\big\|G^\top a\big\|_1 \;=\; a\cdot c-\sum_i |a\cdot g_i| . \] Why: \(a\cdot(c+G\xi)=a\cdot c+\sum_i(a\cdot g_i)\,\xi_i\) with independent \(\xi_i\in[-1,1]\), and each term is least at \(\xi_i=-\operatorname{sign}(a\cdot g_i)\). So “is \(a\cdot x\ge b\) on the whole set?” is one line of arithmetic, no linear program. A nonlinear quantity \(q(x)\ge b\) is checked as its linearization plus a remainder: \(q(c)-\|G^\top\nabla q(c)\|_1-e\ge b\).

Containment

  • In a half-space or a box: exact. \(\mathcal Z(c,G)\) lies in \(\{a\cdot x\le b\}\) exactly when \(a\cdot c+\|G^\top a\|_1\le b\); for a box \([l,u]\) that is \(c-|G|\mathbf 1\ge l\) and \(c+|G|\mathbf 1\le u\).
  • In another zonotope: a sufficient linear test. If \(G_1=G_2\Gamma\), \(c_1-c_2=G_2\beta\) and every row of \([\Gamma,\beta]\) has absolute sum at most 1, then \(\mathcal Z(c_1,G_1)\subseteq\mathcal Z(c_2,G_2)\), since \(c_1+G_1\xi=c_2+G_2(\beta+\Gamma\xi)\) and \(|\beta+\Gamma\xi|\le 1\) componentwise. It is one linear program; exact zonotope containment is hard in general.

Constrained and hybrid zonotopes

A contact guard is a half-space (a gap \(\phi(x)\le 0\)), and a zonotope cut by a half-space is no longer a zonotope. A constrained zonotope \(\{c+G\xi:\ \xi\in[-1,1]^m,\ A\xi=b\}\) is closed under such cuts; its checks become linear programs. At an impact the set splits into branches, one per contact mode. A hybrid zonotope adds binary factors \(\xi^b\in\{-1,1\}^{n_b}\): it is the union of \(2^{n_b}\) constrained zonotopes with shared generators, carried as one set, and its checks are mixed-integer linear programs, one LP per branch when the branches are few.

3. Applied to our certifier

An edge certificate is set reachability. An edge of a funnel is the closed loop \[ x_{k+1}=f\big(x_k,\ \pi(x_k),\ \theta,\ \sigma_k\big), \] with \(x\) the state along the edge, \(\pi\) the feedback law, \(\theta\) the uncertain parameters and \(\sigma_k\) the contact mode. The friction band \(\mu\in[0.3,0.5]\) becomes one extra generator (half-length 0.1 about 0.4) on a state that never changes; scene parameters and the measured disturbances become further generators. The mode is the planned one, and the certificate proves Coulomb’s law selects it: closed contacts stay closed, open ones stay open, normal forces stay positive, sliding forces sit on the cone edge opposite the slip.

Every condition is a closed-form check.

  • the contact-force floor: each closed contact’s normal force at least \(N_{\min}\);
  • the friction cones: \(0.3\,\lambda_n\pm\lambda_t>0\) for a sticking disk, with \(\mu\lambda_n\) for sliding contacts linearized (its bilinear part goes into the remainder);
  • the motor box: four linear checks on the commanded force;
  • the reading condition: each recognizer clause compares a gap, a distance or an angle with its band edge, one scalar check each;
  • progress, slip signs and slip bounds, and the mode’s equations staying nonsingular.

Junctions are containment checks. A junction composes when the exit set certified for one edge lies in the entry set certified for the next. Measured entry sets, joint 95/95 regions of the states MJX actually reaches, are subsets of the executed states and are used only to validate coverage; composition is established on certified sets. The entry set is a design choice: take the previous edge’s certified exit and re-verify the next funnel’s bands from it, and containment holds by construction.

Boxes are the diagonal case. A box exit is a valid zonotope entry as it stands, and a zonotope exit is checked against a box entry exactly. Interval certificates and zonotope certificates therefore compose with no loss at the hand-off, and edges can move to zonotopes one at a time.

The impact window is the first client. Six entry coordinates, five to seven steps, an impact with indeterminate impulses and a contact switch: everything that makes re-boxing fail. The zonotope carries the state through the steps before the impact, splits at the guard into the impact’s branches (constrained zonotopes, or one hybrid zonotope over all of them), and follows each branch to rest.

Learned proposals, checked exactly. A certificate’s free choices (the entry set’s generators, which generators order reduction keeps, how the guard is split) can come from anywhere, including the graph network that is planned to propose certificates per edge. The verifier checks each proposal with the lemmas above. A bad proposal can make a certificate fail; it can never make a false one pass.

4. What the records show so far

One experiment: one impact branch, one \(0.02\times0.02\) mm entry sub-box, and a first-order form (a chain-rule product of interval Jacobians) intersected at every step with the re-boxed set. It is the mean-value form above, not yet generator matrices or the Lagrange form.

  • The make step encloses the reached set 28.8–212.6 times tighter in volume than re-boxing.
  • The steps before the impact gain little.
  • The tail step, alone, is 28.7–212.7 times looser: the product of interval Jacobians overestimates the tail’s stiff rows. The intersection with the re-boxed set carries it.
  • The decision number, 212, is not a shrinkage of the reached set. It is the growth, in volume, of the entry boxes on which every rocking class after the impact is excluded: from 2−4 to 2−2 of the entry box per coordinate, over six coordinates, against a bar of 210 set beforehand.

5. Related work

The foundation. Hybrid zonotopes (Bird, Pangborn, Jain and Koeln, Automatica 2023), their nonlinear successor sets (Siefert et al., ACC 2023), functional decomposition (Siefert et al., arXiv 2304.06827) and the zonoLAB toolbox (Koeln et al., arXiv 2310.15426) are what this route builds on for mode-branched sets; constrained zonotopes (Scott et al., Automatica 2016) are their continuous part.

Methods, not the problem. Zonotopes against the wrapping effect go back to Kühn (Computing, 1998); Lohner’s method is surveyed by Nedialkov, Jackson and Corliss (1999); Girard (HSCC 2005) made zonotopes the reachability set for uncertain linear systems and gave the order reduction used here; affine arithmetic is de Figueiredo and Stolfi (2004).

How this differs from other reachability and funnel work. CORA and polynomial zonotopes, Flow* and Taylor models, and mixed-monotone and immrax interval embeddings compute reachable sets of given dynamics and guards. Polytopic trees with linear containment encodings, mixed-integer planning through contact, LQR-trees and funnel libraries plan with funnels or with modes fixed in the model. Here the contact modes are decided by Coulomb friction over an uncertain friction band and certified along with the funnel; the modes change the object’s contacts with its environment; the funnels are chained on a contact-formation graph and checked against MJX; and learned proposals are verified exactly.
