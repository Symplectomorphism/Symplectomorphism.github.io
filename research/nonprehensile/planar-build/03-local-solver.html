3. The local solver: cost, success and transitions

Critical-path step 3: one success test for every realizer, a hold gate that finds where an edit happens, the compiled cost against the current one, and checkpoints 1–2.

Code. Branch phase1/planar of Symplectomorphism/c-gnn, commits 41b5721 (success predicate, transition sweep), fb792ea (compiled cost, MPPI) and 151ddf4. New: solver/success.py, physics/transition.py, cost/compiler.py, tests/test_step3.py; changed: Cand (H by transition), MPPISolver (a compiled cost, the transition subgoal, the shared success test), PlanarHold (evaluates any pose). Scripts: scripts/03_*.py here.

WarningResult: MPPI does not realize the chain

With the current cost (A), the paper’s compiled cost (B), and three extensions (C, D, E), no variant realizes the pivot or break(gv) (0 of 3 starts each, every variant). The best variants (B and E) realize 12 of the 25 edits that pass H; E also cuts the deepest penetration from 14 mm (A) to 4.7 mm. By the checkpoint rule agreed in Decision 5, the next step is to change the solver before anything else (see the choices).

What step 3 builds

  • One success test for every realizer (solver/success.py), the paper’s Def. success extended to Xiao and Ji’s edits:
    • persistence: every kept contact is on at every sample; for a transfer c → d, one of c and d is on at every sample (the contained contact’s points belong to the larger contact before the change);
    • departure: a contact dropped outright ends above εon;
    • dwell: the recognized contacts equal σ′ for the last Nδ samples, with no tie;
    • penetration: no gap below −εpen;
    • the manipulator horizon, support while the disk lets go, and progress toward the goal for transport.
    The old MPPI test also asked that nothing moves by more than 1 mm during the dwell. That clause is not in the paper; it is now an option, off by default, and the experiment reports the drift instead.
  • The transition sweep (physics/transition.py, Decision 10): H now asks whether the less-constrained end holds at χ₀ or at a nearby pose of the more-constrained formation. Details below.
  • The compiled cost (cost/compiler.py): the paper’s dimensionless terms (band, leave, penetration, control, terminal barrier), extended to transfers by the point-contact view. A transfer keeps the contained contact and moves the released end: for g → gv the other bottom corner lifts until the tilt passes 2θe; for the disk going round a box corner, the contact point slides past the vertex. A constraining transfer (sv → t) lays the edge flat. Compound edges add up their parts. Options: a margin term at the end, a dwell term, the two-phase subgoal (reach χt, then change), and scaling the contact terms by εon instead of the box size.

Where an edit happens: the transition sweep

The edit’s formation σ leaves a few free coordinates. For {mx, gv, sv} they are the tilt and the disk’s height on −xB. The sweep:

  1. finds them as the null space of the contact constraints’ Jacobian (one point constraint per vertex or disk contact, two per edge–edge contact);
  2. lays a grid along them (default: 5 points per direction, ±0.05 in metres, angles times ℓ = a/2);
  3. projects each grid pose back onto the constraints (Gauss–Newton), recognizes it (it must be exactly σ, no tie), and runs the hold LP on the less-constrained end.

H passes if the gate passes at χ₀ or at any grid pose. It returns χt, the nearest passing pose with margin at least γmin = 0.02 (provisional), and also the largest-margin pose. The sweep runs over the more-constrained end (σ for a break, σ′ for a make) and tests statics on the less-constrained one: the geometry of the larger set, the statics of the smaller.

The default grid is coarse (2 held poses against 8), but here it returns the same χt as the finer one. Distances use the paper’s dX with ℓ = a/2 (the box’s half-size), so angles count half as much as with ℓ = a. Tests (test_step3.py):

  • the sweep contains the old gate (wherever χ₀ passes, the sweep passes);
  • every pose the sweep accepts is supported according to an independent float64 LP (HiGHS, with the motor limits);
  • break(gv) at 30° is refused at χ₀ and found near 45°.

The cost experiment

Setup (scripts/03_local_solver.py). MPPI as configured in step2d-base.yaml: 256 samples, 5 updates, controls held 3 steps, 30-step look-ahead. The executed record is 60 + 20 steps (0.8 s): at the configuration’s 20 + 10 steps (0.3 s), a dry run failed every edit for every variant.

  • Edits: the chain of Decision 9, each from its canonical start and from 2 perturbed starts (projected onto the formation, recognized, and offered by Cand), plus break(gv) from 30°, which needs the sweep.
  • The variants:
    • A: the current MPPI cost (hand-set weights; signed linear arrival).
    • B: the paper’s compiled cost.
    • C: B plus the terminal margin term and the dwell term.
    • D: C plus the transition subgoal.
    • E: D with the contact terms divided by εon instead of the box size, a smooth terminal cost, and a barrier of 100.
    B–E use a temperature relative to the samples’ cost spread and an idle input that carries the disk’s weight.
  • Success is the shared test. After a success, the next chain edit is attempted from where the record ended, if Cand offers it there.

GPU (RTX 5080). On CPU the canonical starts give A 1 of 9 and E 4 of 9, the same as on the GPU, at 196 s and 120 s per call (media/step3_experiment_cpu.json).

What the table says

  • The current cost penetrates. A reaches σ′ in 24 of 25 records but succeeds in 3. Its linear arrival term pushes contacts up to 14 mm deep, beyond εpen = 10 mm.
  • The paper’s cost is badly scaled for millimetre contacts. With the box size a = 125 mm as the unit, a 1 mm gap costs (1/125)² ≈ 6·10⁻⁵, so the barrier (10³) decides almost everything. C and D are worse than B, probably because their margin term (on the scale of m g) outweighs those tiny contact terms; the run does not isolate the cause. E, the same terms over εon, recovers B’s success rate with half the penetration and the best follow-on rate.
  • The subgoal changed nothing on the chain. D equals C everywhere. From every canonical start the gate passes at χ₀, so χt = χ₀. Where it matters (break(gv) from 30°), D also fails.
  • What no variant does:
    • The pivot {mx, g, s} → {mx, gv, sv}. B tilts the box to about 30° and reaches σ′ but fails another clause; E never tilts it.
    • break(gv) from 45°. B’s box topples past the corner onto the platform, ending in {mx, t}. E’s stays on its corner at 62–65° and never lifts.
    • break(gv) from 30°. No variant lifts the box.
    These are the edits where the disk must hold the box on a corner through an unstable motion. A 0.3 s look-ahead with 1 N motors does not find that motion.
  • Transport succeeds with B (3 of 3) but not with E (0 of 3, still moving about 20 mm in the dwell window).

Checkpoints 1 and 2

Checkpoint 1: does H prune much? From scripts/03_checkpoints.py. Among candidate edits that are edges and pass Near, the share H rejects:

Nodes Candidates Rejected at χ₀ (step 2) Rejected with the sweep
The chain’s 9 starts 179 18.4% 14.5%
205 demo nodes (every 25th frame, plus jittered copies) 3,112 22.8% 22.7%

By type on the demo nodes:

  • Breaks: H rejects 211 of 280 (75%).
  • Transfers: 444 of 597 (74%).
  • Makes: 47 of 2,022. A make’s less-constrained end is the current formation, which already holds.

So H prunes the edits that drop or shrink support, which are the ones that need it.

Checkpoint 2: does Λ realize the edits that pass H? Not reliably. Every edit in the experiment passes H, and the best variants realize 12 of 25 (48%). The pivot and break(gv) are never realized.

εpen and ties

From scripts/03_ties_eps_pen.py, the step-2 stress test (4,000 jittered demo poses) with εpen replaced:

εpen 1.5 mm 2 mm 3 mm 4 mm 5 mm 10 mm (today)
Tied poses 25 44 98 168 257 475

Nearly all ties have a contact deeper than εon. E’s rollouts penetrate at most 4.7 mm (95th percentile 4.0 mm). An εpen below that would make E’s successes fail the penetration clause.

Choices for review

  1. The solver (checkpoint 2 rule).
    • Recommended: keep E as the cost and change the realizer for the edits that hold the box on a corner. Candidates are contact-implicit MPC and a contact trust region, both admitted by the paper’s Def. lambda.
    • Cheaper first test: a longer look-ahead and more samples for MPPI on the pivot alone, to see whether the failure is the horizon or the method.
  2. The cost variant. Recommended: E, which has the best penetration and follow-on rate and ties B on successes. Rewrite the paper’s Def. cost with contact terms over εon. Drop the terminal margin term as written (C), or rescale it before trying again.
  3. εpen. Recommended: 5 mm. It covers E’s deepest penetration (4.7 mm) and cuts ties from 475 to 257. Stiffer contacts in the simulator (MuJoCo solref) would allow 2–3 mm (44–98 ties). The scene still uses 10 mm until you decide.
  4. The χt rule and γmin. Recommended: the nearest pose with margin at least γmin = 0.1. The nearest pose here (45°, margin 0.25) already clears it. The largest-margin pose moves much further (55° to 63°). γmin is 0.02 for now.
  5. Sweep range and resolution. Recommended: keep 5 points and range 0.05 for now. With ℓ = a/2 the coarse grid returns the same χt as 9 points, at about a third of the cost. With 32 edits per call, Cand takes 0.4–0.5 s per node on CPU. Revisit with the scene families.

The experiment above ran with ℓ = a in dX, before the review aligned the code with the paper (ℓ = a/2). That changes only which held pose the sweep calls nearest, and the goal distance’s angle term, which is zero during transport.
