Nonprehensile Manipulation

Planning through contact: when the planner must invent the contact sequence, not just track one.

When a robot cannot grasp an object, the plan is the sequence of contacts.

Pushing, toppling, pivoting, and re-acquiring an object are all cases in which the robot never closes a rigid grasp. The object moves because contacts are made and broken in the right order, and the hard part of the problem is deciding that order. A trajectory optimizer cannot recover from a contact sequence that was wrong to begin with.

Our work asks a specific question: what should the discrete object be, if the planner is expected to invent contacts that do not exist yet?

The method at a glance

The whole method is one loop: an offline planner turns the task into a chain of certified edges, and online feedback runs them on the plant. Click a block to zoom in.

Results

Certified funnels →: one route of the lift as a chain of certified funnels. Of its 12 segments, 11 are certified, 1 is measured (the rest after the landing impact) and none is uncertified; the goal stop is certified. Executed end to end in MJX, the block reaches the lift goal and comes to rest inside the certified rest set in 1536 of 1536 rollouts; with videos.

The construction has five pieces:

  • Formations. Every body carries a finite, named inventory of features (edges and convex vertices). A candidate contact (a template) is a pair of features on two bodies with a signed gap, defined whether or not the pair touches. A formation is the set of templates in principal contact, in the sense of Xiao and Ji, recognized from a pose with small tolerances; it is the discrete state, carried alongside the continuous pose.
  • Edits. Neighbouring formations differ by making, breaking or transferring one contact; several change at once only when no single change is possible (the pivot). A transport moves the object without changing contacts.
  • The hold gate. Before an edit is tried, a linear program checks that the contacts that persist through it can hold the object against gravity, within the manipulator’s actuator limits, at a pose where the change can happen. In its idealized form it never discards a realizable edit, and an interval version certifies the hold over whole regions of poses.
  • Realization. A local solver, a quasi-static realizer that plans on the edit’s known contacts and tracks the plan in simulation, either realizes the edit or reports why it failed.
  • Search. A search over edits, ordered by a ranker and a value estimate, counts the expensive solver calls, with the number of calls bounded by the ranker’s probabilities.

The hold gate is what forbids a manipulator from letting go mid-topple, and admits the same release once the object rests on other contacts.

Why unmade contacts matter

Complementarity-based planners enumerate velocity modes over the contacts that collision detection has already found, and graphs of convex sets place modes on vertices that must be enumerated in advance. Both are effective for reasoning about contacts a robot already has. Neither can express go and establish contact with that edge, because the contact is not in the state until it exists.

Here every contact the geometry allows is a named template with a gap defined before it touches, so make that contact is an edge of the graph from the start. That is the one structural difference, and everything else in the construction follows from it.

Status

On a proof-of-concept task, lifting a block onto a step with a disk too weak to carry it, the planner finds the lift in which the block is held at every instant, the indirect lift, from the scene and the goal alone in 197 of 200 held-out searches and in all 34 of the original test set (Results). Beside it, one route of the lift is built as a chain of certified funnels, each certified for every disk friction in a declared band and composed on certified sets: from the block at rest through a landing impact certified with zonotopes and a transport into the goal, to a certified stop at the goal. Of its 12 segments, 11 are certified, 1 is measured and none is uncertified. The measured one is the rest after the landing impact: the rest itself is certified on all 16,384 post-impact units by a dissipation (energy) inequality (Background: dissipation certificates), while its link into the transport is still measured. Executed end to end in simulation, the block reaches the lift goal and comes to rest inside the certified rest set in 1536 of 1536 rollouts (Certified funnels). The planar build pages give the details, the Roadmap where it goes next, and Issues the open work.
