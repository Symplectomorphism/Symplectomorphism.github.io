wdm documentation

Weight-dynamics modeling: an energy-balance state-space model of body weight, a joint Kalman filter that learns one person’s metabolism from a scale and a food log, and a model-predictive controller that turns the estimate into a daily calorie and macronutrient prescription.

wdm is a Python package (JAX, Equinox, Diffrax) with a command-line tool of the same name. You weigh in each morning, log what you eat as you go, and once a day it re-estimates where you are and replans what to eat next. The presentation tells the story in slides; these pages are the manual.

How the pieces fit

  • The model has five states: tissue, fat mass, glycogen-bound water, sodium-bound water, and adaptive thermogenesis. Its nine metabolic parameters are personal and unknown.
  • Phase 1 learns how noisy your scale readings are, from weight alone.
  • Phase 2 estimates the states and the parameters together, from weight, food, and an optional weekly waist measurement.
  • The controller plans the next weeks of intake to reach a goal weight by a date (deadline mode) or to lose at a safe steady rate (sustainable mode). Tissue, not scale weight, sits in its objective and safety limits. Only today’s line is a prescription; tomorrow it replans.

Where to start

  • Use it: Getting started installs it and sets up your files; The daily routine is the whole day-to-day obligation (two commands).
  • Look something up: Commands lists every command and option; The observations file and The subject file define every column and setting; Reading the output explains each number the tool prints, what to expect when, and what to do when something looks wrong.
  • Lifting: Exercise energy estimates exercise_kcal from a routine or a workout spreadsheet.
  • Learn the code: the walkthrough runs the package one layer at a time on synthetic data with known truth.
  • Understand the method: the model, estimation, control, their limitations, and the notation.
  • Use it from Python: the API reference, generated from the docstrings; Development covers the layout, the tests, and rebuilding the report and these pages.
WarningNot medical advice

This is a modeling exercise built on population-level physiology and fitted to one person’s self-reported data. Its estimates come with honest, often wide, intervals, and its safety limits are general guidance, not anything specific to you. Talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian before acting on an aggressive plan.
